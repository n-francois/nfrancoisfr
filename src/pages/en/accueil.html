What I believe

Four convictions, drawn from two years of testing and fieldwork.

01

An offer missing from the sources AI engines read becomes invisible

The Paris tourism office is already seeing 25 to 30% less traffic on informational searches. A destination’s visibility no longer depends on its storefront but on its data: structured, up to date, and machine-readable.

02

The human touch may well become travel’s luxury product

As AI spreads, it produces content that all looks alike. The advantage will lie in what it can’t do: welcoming a visitor, telling the story of a place. At Club Med, nine out of ten sales involve human contact.

03

Pragmatism, learning, reversibility

Do what you can, learn, and be ready to pivot: today’s choices will be challenged in six months, and that’s normal. One certainty: structuring quality data will pay off whatever happens.

04

Lumping the four kinds of AI together skews the debate

Automation, classic AI, generative AI, agentic AI: four different logics filed under the same word, with different levels of maturity and different risks. Most debates focus on one of them, then draw conclusions for all four.

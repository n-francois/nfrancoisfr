What I believe

Four convictions shaped by my experience, my tests, and feedback from the field.

01

A destination’s value lies in what Google doesn’t know

Opening hours, addresses, phone numbers… like it or not, AI tools already pull them from Google, Booking, or Tripadvisor. Destinations have every reason to focus on what these platforms don’t know well, such as actual access conditions or seasonal constraints. That’s where they can stay one step ahead, provided they structure this knowledge.

02

The human touch may well become travel’s luxury product

As AI spreads, it produces content that looks increasingly alike. Once it becomes commonplace, the difference will lie in what it can’t do, in what can’t be automated… like welcoming a visitor or telling the story of a place with sincerity and lived experience.

03

Pragmatism, learning, reversibility

I strongly believe in these three values, in this exact order. First, be pragmatic, because waiting for every certainty means never moving forward. Then, learn by doing. And be willing to backtrack. Decisions made today, in an extremely fast-moving context, will inevitably be called into question within three to six months. One thing is certain: structuring quality data remains useful whatever happens.

04

Before talking about AI, know which one you mean

Automation, classic AI, generative AI, agentic AI… Four approaches that often get lumped under the same word. Yet they are very different, with nowhere near the same maturity, the same power, or, above all, the same risks. We often talk about generative AI, then map what we say about it onto the others…

Written in September 2026. This will evolve, inevitably. I explore these ideas every month in my newsletter (opens in a new tab), in French.
